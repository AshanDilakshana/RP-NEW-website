Executive Summary

Research Abstract

Short-term weather forecasting for accurate aviation weather prediction is significant in ensuring the safe and efficient operation of airports, especially in Sri Lanka where atmospheric conditions change sharply in accordance with monsoon seasons: Southwest Monsoon, Northeast Monsoon, First Inter-Monsoon, and Second Inter-Monsoon. In this research, a hyper-local aviation weather forecasting and verification system is introduced for Bandaranaike International Airport (VCBI, Katunayake). Four specialized machine learning subsystems are developed to predict surface wind components, temperature, atmospheric pressure (QNH), cloud status, visibility, dew point, and relative humidity at a 3-hour lookahead horizon (T+3h). Predictions are physically converted into operational aviation indicators including Density Altitude (DA), Runway 04 Headwind and Crosswind, and Radiation Fog hazard warnings. Every forecast is automatically verified against future official METAR observations using MAE, RMSE, and R² metrics to provide continuous validation and handle seasonal concept drift.

Context & Motivation

Importance for Aviation in Sri Lanka

Sri Lanka is a tropical island situated in the northern Indian Ocean, subject to complex seasonal monsoon reversals and convective thunderstorm activities. Bandaranaike International Airport (VCBI) handles the nation's primary international passenger and cargo operations.

Commercial aircraft operations, runway throughput, fuel planning, and takeoff weight limits are directly governed by localized surface weather parameters—namely crosswind gusts, air density, cloud base, and runway visibility. Unexpected wind shifts and rapid visibility drops pose significant safety risks and lead to costly holding patterns or diversions.

The Research Problem

The Hyper-Local Research Challenge

Conventional Numerical Weather Prediction (NWP) models such as GFS and ECMWF operate on horizontal grid cells typically 15–25 km wide. A single grid cell averages the airport runway, the nearby Negombo lagoon, the Indian Ocean coastline, and inland terrain together, failing to capture microscale boundary-layer convective developments.

Furthermore, existing Sri Lankan meteorological studies focus on broad regional rainfall prediction rather than aerodrome-specific short-term (T+3 hours) forecasting and automated verification against real-time METAR data.

Project Scope

Main Research Objectives

The project was systematically conducted through four interrelated primary goals:

1. Hyper-Local Data Engineering & Preprocessing

Curate, clean, chronologically order, and engineer physically informed features (cyclic angular time and wind encodings, dew-point depressions, rolling trends, and interaction variables) from over 120,000 hourly historical VCBI meteorological observations (2019–2026).

2. Multi-Model Seasonal Machine Learning

Design, tune, and evaluate tailored machine learning algorithms for distinct parameters: Hybrid RF+LSTM for temperature/pressure, XGBoost + Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) for wind, XGBoost for cloud/visibility, and LightGBM with August-Roche-Magnus formula for QNH, dew point, and RH.

3. Aeronautical Physics & Safety Layer

Translate raw numerical weather predictions into operational aeronautical metrics: Pressure Altitude and Density Altitude (Normal/Elevated/Critical), Runway 04 Headwind and Crosswind vectors against ICAO tolerances, and radiation fog thermodynamic hazard warnings.

4. Automated Forecast Verification & MLOps

Implement an automated pipeline that continuously pulls live hourly METAR observations from the Aviation Weather Center (AWC), executes forward-looking predictions, and objectively verifies forecasts against ground truth using MAE, RMSE, and R².

Modular AI Architecture

The Four Research Forecasting Subsystems

Each team researcher developed a dedicated subsystem aligned with a distinct atmospheric parameter and monsoon characteristic:

Northeast Monsoon

Temperature & Pressure Model

Researcher: Sankadeepa W.D.I. (IT22113986)

Architecture: Hybrid Random Forest (RF) + Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)

Combines non-linear ensemble decision trees for multi-variable interactions with recurrent memory cells for 6-hour sequential dependencies. Output directly computes Density Altitude to monitor thin-air takeoff performance.

Achieved Hybrid R²: 0.84 | Hybrid MAE: ~0.86
Southwest Monsoon

Wind Patterns & Pilot Portal

Researcher: Ranasinghe R.A.A.D. (IT22269720)

Architecture: XGBoost + Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT)

Employs circular sine/cosine angle transformations. XGBoost generates rapid 1-hour nowcasts, while TFT leverages attention mechanisms for 3-hour forecasts, converted to Runway 04 headwind and crosswind vectors.

Ensemble Wind MAE: <1.00 kt | Angular MAE: 10.6°
First Inter-Monsoon

Cloud & Visibility Model

Researcher: Jayawardhana H.M.S.I. (IT22619976)

Architecture: Gradient Boosted Trees (XGBoost)

Leverages dew point depression, wind-humidity, and pressure interaction features to predict aerodrome cloud coverage classification and horizontal visibility in meters, serving predictions via a lightweight API.

Cloud Accuracy: 78.15% | Cloud/Visibility MAE: 347.7
Second Inter-Monsoon

QNH, Dew Point & Relative Humidity

Researcher: Jayathilaka P.K.V.K. (IT22069504)

Architecture: LightGBM + August-Roche-Magnus Physical Formula

Combines fast gradient boosted regression for barometric pressure (QNH) and dew point with thermodynamic formulations to derive Relative Humidity while enforcing physical consistency and radiation fog alerts.

Dew Point R²: 0.66 | Dew Point MAE: 0.58 °C | QNH MAE: 1.8 hPa

1. Operational Forecaster Workspace

Integrates live METAR telemetry, aerodrome operational status, and real-time sensor updates for Bandaranaike International Airport (VCBI / Colombo FIR), synchronized to both Sri Lanka Standard Time (SLST) and Coordinated Universal Time (UTC).

Allows on-duty meteorological officers to trigger forward 3-hour forecasts using trained monsoonal ML pipelines, instantly predicting boundary-layer changes before routine 3-hourly TAF publication.
